Outer steps and accuracy

How each model's move accuracy changes with the number of outer thinking steps, on the same positions, for the two adaptive-D seeds, the credit-window variants C and A, and the four fixed-step controls.

What the step analysis shows

    Figure 1

    Accuracy at each outer step

    Every model is forced to answer after step k, for each k it can run. Circles mark where each adaptive model actually stops on average with its own halting rule. A fixed-step model was trained to answer only at its last step, so its earlier points show partial computation, not a usable shorter model.

    Figure 2

    Accuracy against compute

    The halting rule stops when the model's cumulative stop probability crosses a threshold; the trained rule uses 0.5. Sweeping that threshold from 0.05 to 0.95 trades steps for accuracy without retraining. Squares are the fixed-step controls, each with its one setting.

    Figure 3

    Shallow and deep positions

    Positions split by the depth of the forced win the best move needs. Shallow means a forced win in 1 to 4 moves; deep means 9 or more. Extra outer steps matter almost only on deep positions.

    Figure 4

    Where each model stops, by depth

    Share of positions in each depth band that stop at each outer step, using the model's own rule. Darker means more positions.

    Figure 5

    Does the per-position stop decision help?

    Each adaptive model is compared with itself: once with its own stopping rule, and once forced to run the same number of steps on every position, rounded to its average. This separates what the stop decision adds at test time from what the halting objective added during training.

    Method

    How these numbers were made
    • Every model was evaluated on the same positions: 12,991 tactical positions with a known forced-win depth, and 10,000 general positions sampled from play. The comparisons are therefore paired position by position.
    • "Accuracy" means the chosen move is one of the optimal moves according to the solver.
    • An adaptive model stops at the first step where its cumulative stop probability reaches 0.5. "Forced to answer at step k" reads the move prediction at step k whatever the halting head says.
    • Each fixed control was trained to always run k steps and is graded at step k. It has no halting head.
    • All runs: 22,000 updates, one cosine schedule, commit cbae5fb. Adaptive D has two seeds; every other model has one seed, so differences under about 1 point between single-seed models may reflect seed noise more than design.
